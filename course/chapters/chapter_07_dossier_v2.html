Welcome to chapter seven. In the old edition this was “The Ten-Part Dossier.” That structure was a useful start, but it is no longer the standard. A serious research question has more than ten jobs to do.

The current standard is the thirty-section dossier, codified as Sabre-Tek Dossier Benchmark v1. Thirty is a floor for a full dossier, not a target to pad toward. If the evidence cannot support thirty substantive sections, the right answer is a shorter, honest document — not invented content.

The sections are grouped into eight jobs:

  • Identity — disease, gene, protein, inheritance, synonyms, diagnostic context and clinical burden.
  • Mechanism — loss of function, gain of function, aggregation, haploinsufficiency, splicing, metabolism and the evidence for each.
  • Structure — experimental structures, constructs, coverage, quality and what the model does not cover.
  • Evidence base — primary literature, curated databases, trials, natural history, conflicts and gaps.
  • Therapeutic landscape — the routes that are plausible, what has been tried, what failed and what is approved.
  • Trial and patient context — phase, endpoints, biomarkers, prior lines, inclusion and exclusion, as research context only.
  • Critical appraisal — the strongest objections, the red team, the falsifiers and the missing data.
  • Decision — the recommendation, alternatives, conditions, what would change it and the next experiment.

Each section has three depth elements: a key finding, at least one paragraph of analysis, and a falsifier — the evidence that would change the conclusion. A section without a falsifier is a summary, not an argument.

The benchmark requires at least six factual figures, six quantitative tables, thirty unique sources, twenty-five claims and eight red-team items. Figures are diagrams that carry information, not decoration. Tables are comparisons with numbers, not lists of drug names. Every figure and table carries its own sources. If a section has an exhibit that already carries the citations, the section-level source line is suppressed — one citation block per exhibit, no source-only pages.

The document has a cover, a document-control block, a contents page and a method page. It states its version, date, scope and next review. It has a running footer, page numbers, headings that stay with their content and tables that keep their header rows. The layout is part of the evidence: a document that cannot be read or printed has failed before the science is assessed.

The five reference dossiers are Dengue, Friedreich ataxia, NSCLC, Gaucher and Fabry. They are the current benchmark, not a claim that every disease has the same evidence. The public release page is a summary; the full dossier, claim ledger and evidence room are access-controlled. The public page must never pretend to be the full product.
