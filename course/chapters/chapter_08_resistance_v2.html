Welcome to chapter eight. Everything we have built so far assumes that a disease can be understood, targeted and measured. Resistance is the reason that assumption is never the end of the story.

Resistance is not one mechanism. It is a family of ways a disease escapes a treatment. A cancer can mutate the target, amplify a bypass pathway, change its identity, or simply stop depending on the protein we thought was essential. A rare disease can progress through a different cell type, a different compartment, or a different biological process than the one we modelled.

The EGFR story is the clearest example in the current work. First-line osimertinib is a strong benchmark for sensitising EGFR mutations. Resistance to it is heterogeneous: C797S, T790M loss, MET amplification, HER2 amplification, PI3K pathway activation and histological transformation. C797S in cis with T790M is not the same problem as C797S in trans. There is no single post-osimertinib standard. A research programme that does not name the resistance cohort is testing a slogan, not a hypothesis.

The old edition of this chapter framed resistance as a problem that targeted degradation would solve. That was too narrow. Degradation is one route. It may be useful when the problem is the protein itself. It is not automatically useful when the resistance is a bypass pathway, a downstream mutation, or a change in cell state. The mechanism has to match the resistance, not the other way round.

The practical consequences are:

  • Re-biopsy or liquid biopsy at progression, because the resistance mechanism may have changed.
  • Name the cohort before naming the molecule: C797S, MET amplification, T790M loss, transformation or another driver.
  • Separate PFS from OS. A progression-free survival gain can justify a trial; it is not automatically a survival benefit.
  • Treat CNS disease as its own endpoint domain. A systemic response does not prove an intracranial response.
  • Record the failures. Terminated programmes and negative trials are part of the evidence, not an embarrassment to be omitted.

Resistance is also a reason to keep the dossier alive. A claim that was current at one review can become stale after a readout. The daily watchlist exists to surface those changes; the claim ledger exists to show what changed and when. A static PDF is a snapshot, not a living record.
