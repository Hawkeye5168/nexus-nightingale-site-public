Welcome to the final chapter. We have travelled from a chain of amino acids to a source-linked dossier. Now I want to be plain about what is still hard.

The first hard problem is ab initio folding. Supervised folding reconstructs a known structure and is the production path when a reference exists. Ab initio folding starts from sequence alone and is still the research frontier. It is not solved for large proteins without deep evolutionary data, and we do not pretend otherwise. The current work is a research preview, not a blind-prediction claim.

The second is MSA depth. The multiple sequence alignment is the evolutionary evidence behind a contact map. If the alignment is shallow, the contact map is weak. The current catalogue is dominated by shallow alignments; that is a real constraint, not a footnote. Improving alignment depth is more valuable than adding another confident diagram.

The third is the gap between design and experiment. A designed molecule is not a medicine. A predicted structure is not a measurement. A dossier is not a clinical trial. The EGFR degrader record is research-grade; the AUTOTAC/linker corpus is not production-ready; the five benchmark dossiers are evidence products, not cures. Those statements belong in the document, not in a private caveat.

The fourth is translation. A research dossier can help a scientist ask a better question, choose a resistance cohort, or design a cleaner experiment. It cannot establish that a patient will benefit. That requires wet-lab work, clinical trials, regulatory review and independent expertise.

The fifth is scale without dilution. The catalogue can hold 11,645 entities and the pipeline can run 4,245 gene-associated analyses, but depth is not the same as breadth. Five benchmark dossiers with 30–31 sections, 30–33 sources and 25–28 claims each are more useful than a thousand thin summaries. The next stage is to deepen the strongest cases and put them in front of people who will attack them.

So where does that leave the project? It leaves us with a working research instrument: a fourteen-stage pipeline, a twenty-four-source evidence layer, a thirty-section dossier standard, a daily review loop and a public record that states its limits. It is not a cure machine. It is a way to organise evidence, expose uncertainty and design the next experiment.

That is the work. The dossier is not the answer; it is the instrument that helps a scientist find one.
