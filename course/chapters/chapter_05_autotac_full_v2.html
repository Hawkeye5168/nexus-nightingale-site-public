Welcome to chapter five. This is the chapter that used to be called “AUTOTAC in Full.” I have changed the title because the honest story is bigger than one molecule, and because the old version made the route sound more mature than it is.

Targeted degradation is a simple idea with a hard execution. Instead of blocking a protein, you remove it. The cell already has machinery for destroying proteins; the drug borrows that machinery and points it at the target.

There are two main removal routes. The proteasome is a barrel-shaped machine that shreds proteins tagged with ubiquitin. It is fast and precise, but it has a size limit: large proteins and aggregates do not fit. Autophagy is a bulk-clearance system that can engulf larger cargo, including aggregates, and deliver it to the lysosome. It is less selective by default, which is exactly why a degrader has to supply the selectivity.

A degrader is usually bifunctional: one end binds the target, the other recruits a destruction component, and a linker connects them. PROTACs recruit an E3 ubiquitin ligase. AUTOTACs use an autophagy-targeting ligand. Molecular glues achieve a similar effect with a single small molecule by creating a new protein–protein interface.

The appeal is obvious. If the target is a scaffolding protein, a mutant that has lost its drug pocket, or a protein that needs to be removed rather than inhibited, degradation can reach places a simple inhibitor cannot.

But the appeal is also where the overclaiming starts. A designed degrader is not a drug because it has three named parts. It has to show:

  • productive target engagement in the relevant cell and compartment;
  • selectivity against closely related proteins;
  • a linker and warhead that are chemically stable and synthetically accessible;
  • cellular degradation, not just binding;
  • a functional consequence that helps the disease model;
  • exposure at a dose that does not destroy the therapeutic window.

The current AUTOTAC/linker corpus is a research prototype. The 10 November linker audit flagged plausible-looking chemistry, uncertain molecular weights, wrong E3 ligands and citations that do not support the sentence. That is exactly why the pipeline labels the record research-grade and does not present it as a candidate. A beautiful three-part diagram is not a chemistry package.

The same caution applies to the EGFR degrader story. EGFR is a genuine oncogenic target, and degradation is a rational research direction. But the approved first-line route is a tyrosine-kinase inhibitor, not a degrader. A degrader must beat the existing therapeutic index, not just remove more protein on a western blot.

So the rule for this chapter is: degradation is a route, not a result. It becomes a programme only when the target, the resistance cohort, the exposure, the selectivity and the safety package are named. Until then, it belongs in the research column.
