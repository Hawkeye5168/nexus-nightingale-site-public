Welcome to chapter nine. We have built a folding engine, a mechanism gate, a modality map, a twenty-four-source evidence layer and a thirty-section dossier. None of it matters if the system is allowed to sound more certain than its evidence.

This chapter is about the machinery that stops that from happening.

The first rule is the TJ Compassion Protocol. TJ is not an acronym. TJ is a real person — the son of Karen, Papa’s partner — and the protocol exists because of him. The test is simple: would Karen be proud to show this to TJ? If the answer is yes, ship it. If it is no, fix it. If it is unclear, verify it.

That is not a slogan. It is a design constraint. It means we do not hide a failed experiment, a shallow alignment, a negative trial or an unknown mechanism. It means a refusal is a legitimate output. It means the system must never invent a target, a citation or a number to make a page look complete.

The second rule is source discipline. Every claim resolves to a source ID, a URL and a retrieval date. Every source has a tier. A primary trial is not a registry entry; a registry entry is not a preprint; a model output is not a measurement. If a source cannot be resolved, the claim is marked, not decorated.

The third rule is falsifiability. Every section carries a falsifier: the evidence that would change its conclusion. Every hypothesis carries a critical experiment. If we cannot say what result would prove us wrong, we do not have a hypothesis. We have a hope.

The fourth rule is release gates. A dossier is not published because it looks finished. It passes the source gate, the evidence gate, the safety gate and the scope gate. The audit checks the section count, the figures, the tables, the sources, the claims, the red-team items and the depth blocks. A failed audit is fixed, not bypassed.

The fifth rule is versioning. A dossier is dated and versioned. A new paper or trial enters a review queue; it does not silently rewrite a claim. When the evidence changes, the version changes and the changelog says why. A reader can see what was known on the day the document was issued.

Those five rules are the difference between a research product and a confident search result. They are also the reason the public pages carry scope notes: research use only, not clinical advice, not a diagnosis, not a regulatory submission.
