Research Exchange · founding cohort

Find the person who can test the question.

A consent-based exchange for clinicians, scientists and research organisations working on EGFR-mutant NSCLC resistance. No scraped emails. No patient data. Introductions by relay.

Why

Resistance is a team problem

The oncologist sees the cohort. The pathologist sees the tissue. The structural biologist sees the pocket. The ctDNA lab sees the heterogeneity. The dossier is useful only if those conversations can start.

Who

Clinicians and scientists

Thoracic oncologists, molecular pathologists, structural and computational biologists, medicinal chemists, ctDNA groups, patient organisations and access researchers.

What

One question at a time

C797S structural biology · MET assay concordance · ctDNA cohort definition · post-osimertinib access and equity · candidate modality review.

How

Opt in, then relay

Publish a short professional profile only with explicit consent. Interested parties request an introduction; Sabre-Tek relays it. Either side can withdraw.

STEP 01

Express interest

Send your public profile and the collaboration you want.

STEP 02

Check affiliation

We confirm the public institutional page or ORCID. No private email is published.

STEP 03

Publish with consent

A short profile: role, institution, interests, collaboration sought, conflict statement.

STEP 04

Relay introduction

We connect the two parties. No lead lists, no scraping, no automatic sharing.
