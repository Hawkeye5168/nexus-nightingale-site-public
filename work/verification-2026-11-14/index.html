Source verification update · 14 November 2026

What we checked, and what we corrected.

A source-level review of all eighteen disease dossiers: registry identifiers, OMIM entries, source links, structural constructs and named treatment labels. This is a research record, not a clinical validation.

The short version

The check was deliberately adversarial: every disease identity, registry number, OMIM entry and structural citation was compared with the source it claims to use. Where the record was wrong, the dossier was corrected and versioned rather than quietly patched.

Registry and OMIM

  • Orphanet codes were checked for the fifteen dossiers that have one. NSCLC is not an orphan disease; FNIP1 deficiency and MAPT-related tauopathy have no Orphanet code in the sources used.
  • OMIM entries were opened directly in a logged-in browser for all seventeen dossiers with an OMIM entity. NSCLC has no OMIM disease entry.
  • Sickle cell disease: ORPHA:232 is sickle cell anaemia; the broader disease group is ORPHA:275752. Corrected in SCD-001 v1.2.
  • Gaucher disease: OMIM 230800 is the type I entry. Type II is 230900 and type III is 231000. Corrected in GBA1-006 v2.1.
  • FNIP1 deficiency: the October source report conflated it with the separate condition called Fields disease. The dossier is FNIP1-001; the old Fields page is a correction notice.

Source links

Ten rebuild dossiers: 300 of 300 source URLs returned a live page after repair. Eight earlier dossiers: 225 of 244 returned a live page; the other 19 are publisher or agency bot walls (OMIM, NEJM, Annals, CDC, Wiley/Elsevier/DOI), not dead links. Five genuinely broken links were replaced: four in SCD and one in SMA.

Vendor search URLs were replaced with stable landing pages, so a citation cannot rot into a search query. Every PDB citation now says what the deposit actually is.

What this does not mean

This is not clinical validation. It is a source and identity check. Independent clinical or regulatory review remains outstanding. No patient data are used. No efficacy claim is made. The dossiers are research-use documents, not medical advice.

The L.O.V.E. protocol is the method used here: Learn, Observe, Verify, Explain — pronounced “love”, because we care enough to be thorough and honest. TJ Compassion is the reason; L.O.V.E. is the how.
