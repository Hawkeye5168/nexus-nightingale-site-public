Model assurance positive control · MIT-001 · 16 November 2026

Ventricular ectopy classifier

Record-held-out on MIT-BIH. A normal-baseline calibrated control that passes at a declared 5% budget — and reports that the 1% target is not met.

Record-held-out, not a clinical validation. 48 MIT-BIH records from 47 subjects; 201/202 grouped; record 232 has no N/V beats, so the V-versus-N evaluation uses 47 records and 46 groups. No patient names; no clinical review. The threshold uses a labelled normal baseline from each held-out record, and those calibration beats are excluded from the evaluation.

Declared budget: 5%. The 1% calibration threshold gives 4.18% actual FPR, inside the 5% budget, at 94.1% TPR. The 1% target itself is not met and is not claimed. A single global threshold gives 18.4% actual FPR at the 1% target, which is why the control uses a per-record normal baseline. Null AUC is 0.493; its detection is 5.0% at its own 5.1% false-positive rate, so the signal collapses.

Why this control exists

The earlier BC-001 control was sample-level and could not answer a reviewer’s patient-grouping objection. MIT-001 is split by record/patient group and calibrated on a normal baseline; it is the positive control that answers that objection without pretending to be a clinical validation.

Not a clinical claim. This is a V-versus-N research control, not a diagnosis, treatment recommendation, regulatory submission or evidence of patient benefit. Other arrhythmia symbols are excluded.
