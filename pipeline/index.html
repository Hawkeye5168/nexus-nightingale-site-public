Sabre-Tek · NEXUS Nightingale

From a target to a cited dossier

A complete computational drug-discovery pipeline, described by what it does — fourteen stages, four phases, and the evidence depth behind each one. Not by the machinery underneath.

Research use only. NEXUS is a computational research platform. It is not clinically validated, has no regulatory approval, and does not provide diagnosis, treatment or clinical advice. Every output requires independent experimental and clinical validation.

Phase I · Stage 01

Intake and identity

Start with a protein target, a UniProt accession, a sequence, a gene, or a disease. NEXUS resolves the identity against public reference data, records what it knows, and fails loudly when an identifier cannot be resolved rather than guessing.

  • Target and sequence resolution
  • Gene, disease and organism context
  • Structured research record
Phase I · Stage 02

Evidence assembly

The pipeline gathers public evidence from biomedical databases and registries, then binds each statement to a source and an evidence tier. The result is an evidence chain, not an unsourced summary.

  • Disease, gene, structure and trial evidence
  • Source identifiers and retrieval dates
  • Evidence tiers and explicit gaps
Phase I · Stage 03

Research record

The question becomes a structured record: what is known, what is uncertain, what is missing, and what would count as an answer.

  • Decision question and countercase
  • Known / uncertain / missing
  • Falsifiers and next experiment
Phase II · Stage 04

Supervised structural recovery

Where an experimental reference exists, the supervised path recovers a structure against it. The result is labelled as reconstruction, not blind prediction, and the reference construct is named.

  • Reference PDB and construct
  • TM-score and RMSD
  • Limitations recorded
Phase II · Stage 05

Research folding path

A separate research path attempts a fold without a template. It is reported separately, is not the production path, and is never presented as a validated structure prediction.

  • Research-preview only
  • No production claim
  • Failures reported
Phase II · Stage 06

Domains and pockets

Locate domains, interfaces and binding cavities before any design begins. Experimental and predicted structural evidence are kept separate.

  • Domain boundaries
  • Pocket and interface context
  • Confidence and provenance
Phase III · Stage 07

Mechanism gate

Gain of function may be degraded; loss of function is protected. The refusal is explicit: no degrader is issued where the mechanism would make the patient worse.

  • Mechanism classification
  • Go / no-go gate
  • Explicit refusal where appropriate
Phase III · Stage 08

Modality routing

Choose among therapeutic routes: small molecule, biologic, gene therapy, editing, RNA-directed approach, protein replacement, cell therapy or symptomatic care.

  • Mechanism-aware route
  • Multiple modalities
  • Research-needed flag
Phase III · Stage 09

Candidate design

Where the mechanism supports a design, record the target-engagement hypothesis, proposed readout, safety questions and falsifier. Designs are research hypotheses, not medicines.

  • Candidate routes
  • Target engagement hypothesis
  • Critical experiment
Phase III · Stage 10

Safety and developability gates

Before anything leaves the research environment, it passes citation, evidence, safety and scope checks. Unsupported claims and clinical overreach are rejected by design.

  • Citation and source gate
  • Safety and developability
  • Scope and compassion review
Phase IV · Stage 11

Knowledge assembly

Bring the biomedical databases and registries into one evidence chain, with provenance and retrieval dates. A count is coverage, not efficacy.

  • Source-linked evidence chain
  • Provenance and dates
  • Explicit gaps
Phase IV · Stage 12

Patient and trial context

Where appropriate, rank recruiting trials against variant, therapy line, biomarkers and safety profile — as research context, not clinical advice.

  • Trial landscape
  • Biomarker and line context
  • No eligibility decision
Phase IV · Stage 13

Verify and report

Run the evidence and scope gates, then produce a cited report with its limitations attached. The scientist reviews and challenges it before release.

  • Claim ledger
  • Audit and source checks
  • Limitations stated
Phase IV · Stage 14

Dossier and review

The output is a cited dossier: disease, gene, evidence, hypotheses, candidate routes, trials, gaps and next experiment. It is a research product, not a clinical recommendation.

  • Decision dossier
  • Review questions
  • Read-only trust boundary

The source register is unique, every claim is status-marked, and every section states what would change its conclusion. New papers and trials are scanned daily and enter a review queue; they do not silently rewrite a claim.

Coverage counts describe the evidence base, not efficacy. The five benchmark dossiers are research-use documents, not clinical validation.

What the pipeline gives a scientist

A structured starting point: what is known, what is uncertain, what is missing, which hypotheses are testable, and which experiment would move the field. The pipeline organises evidence and designs; the scientist sets the question and validates the answer.
