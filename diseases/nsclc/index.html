02What it is

Non-small cell lung cancer is a heterogeneous group of thoracic cancers. EGFR-mutant NSCLC is a molecular subset, not a single clinical entity: it is defined by an activating mutation in the EGFR receptor tyrosine kinase, most commonly an exon 19 deletion or the exon 21 L858R substitution.

EGFR mutation prevalence is geography-dependent. A 2021 meta-analysis of advanced NSCLC reported approximately 12.8% in Europe and 49.1% in Asia, with histology and testing practice also affecting the observed proportion. The practical consequence is that testing policy changes the population a programme can reach. A jurisdiction with broad reflex testing will identify more eligible patients than one with selective testing, even where the underlying biology is the same.

This page describes the evidence and the research route. It is not a diagnosis, a treatment recommendation or an eligibility decision. Treatment decisions belong to a qualified clinical team.

03What goes wrong

Activating mutations in EGFR exons 18–21 drive constitutive kinase activity and downstream survival signalling. L858R and exon 19 deletion are both sensitising, but they are different alleles with different biology and subgroup behaviour; pooling them can hide the difference a programme is trying to exploit.

Acquired resistance to osimertinib is heterogeneous. EGFR-dependent mechanisms include C797S and T790M loss; EGFR-independent mechanisms include MET amplification, HER2 amplification, PI3K pathway activation and histological transformation. C797S in cis with T790M is not the same problem as C797S in trans. There is no single post-osimertinib standard. A research programme must name the resistance cohort before it names the molecule.

Structural note: the PDB entries used by the dossier are kinase-domain constructs, not the full receptor in a cell: 1M17 (wild-type kinase + erlotinib, 2.6 Å), 1XKK (wild-type + lapatinib, 2.4 Å), 5UG9 (L858R/T790M/V948R + covalent inhibitor, 1.33 Å) and 4ZAU (wild-type + AZD9291, 2.8 Å). None establishes in vivo selectivity or a clinical effect.

04How NIGHTINGALE routes it

Approved route: a small-molecule EGFR tyrosine-kinase inhibitor — osimertinib is the first-line benchmark for classic sensitising mutations.

This is an oncogenic gain-of-function target, but the approved route is inhibition, not degradation. Mutant-selective degradation, antibody–drug conjugates and combination strategies are research routes. The current EGFR degrader record is research-grade and is not a production candidate. NIGHTINGALE does not convert a research route into a treatment recommendation.

05Treatment today

Osimertinib (Tagrisso)

FDA first-line approval 18 April 2018
Third-generation EGFR tyrosine-kinase inhibitor · first-line metastatic EGFR exon 19 deletion or exon 21 L858R

Osimertinib is the first-line benchmark for classic sensitising EGFR-mutant advanced NSCLC. The FLAURA trial enrolled 556 patients: median progression-free survival was 18.9 months versus 10.2 months with gefitinib or erlotinib (HR 0.46), and final median overall survival was 38.6 versus 31.8 months (HR 0.80; p = 0.046).

FLAURA2 enrolled 557 patients and combined osimertinib with platinum-pemetrexed: median progression-free survival was 25.5 versus 16.7 months (HR 0.62). Overall survival was immature at the primary analysis, and the combination adds chemotherapy toxicity. AURA3 addressed T790M-positive disease after prior EGFR TKI: median progression-free survival was 10.1 versus 4.4 months (HR 0.30); final overall survival was 26.8 versus 22.5 months (HR 0.87; not statistically significant), with substantial crossover.

Osimertinib is generally better tolerated than cytotoxic chemotherapy, but it causes diarrhoea, rash, paronychia, QT prolongation, cardiomyopathy and interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis. The label carries these warnings. A survival or progression-free survival gain must be weighed against that burden. FDA approval is not a NICE recommendation or a guarantee of access in every health system; access is jurisdiction-specific.

What this page does not do: it does not provide dose management, eligibility criteria or an individual treatment plan. Those require a qualified clinical team and the current local label.

06In the pipeline

  • Resistance-directed therapy. C797S, MET amplification, T790M loss and bypass pathways require re-biopsy and a matched agent; there is no single post-osimertinib sequence.
  • CNS disease. Brain metastases are a distinct efficacy domain. CNS penetration is necessary but not sufficient; the endpoint must be intracranial disease control.
  • Antibody–drug conjugates. Investigational in EGFR-mutant NSCLC; early response signals do not establish a front-line or combination role.
  • Immunotherapy boundary. Single-agent checkpoint inhibition is not established for EGFR-mutant disease; KEYNOTE-024 excluded sensitising EGFR mutations and ALK translocations.
  • Early-stage disease. Adjuvant and neoadjuvant EGFR TKI strategies need event-free or disease-free survival evidence; metastatic response does not establish adjuvant benefit.
  • Degraders. A mutant-selective EGFR degrader is a research hypothesis, not a clinical candidate. It must show productive target–E3 ligase engagement, mutant selectivity, brain exposure where relevant, and a therapeutic index.
