02What it is

Prostate cancer is a heterogeneous disease of the prostate gland. It ranges from low-grade, low-volume disease that may never cause harm to aggressive, metastatic disease. The clinical state matters: localised, hormone-sensitive, non-metastatic castration-resistant and metastatic castration-resistant disease have different evidence and different decisions.

The androgen receptor is a major therapeutic axis. AR is a 920-amino-acid nuclear receptor with an N-terminal domain, DNA-binding domain, hinge and ligand-binding domain. Androgen binding activates the receptor and drives AR target genes. AR-directed therapy is not the same as curing the disease.

The best-characterised structure in the pipeline is PDB 1GS4, a 248-residue ligand-binding-domain mutant construct. It is not the full receptor. A construct is not a protein.

03What goes wrong

Prostate cancer contains recurrent genomic alterations: TMPRSS2–ERG fusions, PTEN loss and PI3K pathway activation, and homologous-recombination repair defects including BRCA2. Mismatch-repair deficiency is rare but actionable with tumour-agnostic immunotherapy.

Resistance to AR-directed therapy can arise through AR amplification, AR mutations, AR splice variants such as AR-V7, glucocorticoid-receptor signalling, neuroendocrine differentiation and bypass pathways. No single mechanism explains all progression.

A genomic alteration is not automatically a treatment indication. The variant, disease state, prior therapy and evidence level all matter.

05Treatment today — the shape, not a prescription

Localised disease is managed with active surveillance, radical prostatectomy, external-beam radiotherapy or brachytherapy according to risk. Metastatic hormone-sensitive disease is intensified with androgen deprivation therapy plus an AR pathway inhibitor and/or docetaxel in eligible patients. Non-metastatic castration-resistant disease is treated with an AR pathway inhibitor. Metastatic castration-resistant disease includes taxanes, AR pathway inhibitors, PARP inhibitors for HRR-altered disease, radium-223, sipuleucel-T in selected patients and Lu-177 PSMA-617 in PSMA-positive disease.

These are categories, not a sequence. Trial populations are selected; access varies by jurisdiction; a licensed medicine is not automatically available. NICE TA1110 and TA1109 were checked as current UK records, but a complete access assessment has not been completed.
