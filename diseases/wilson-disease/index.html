02What it is

Wilson disease is an autosomal recessive disorder of copper transport caused by ATP7B deficiency, leading to toxic copper accumulation in the liver, brain, and cornea. It presents with liver disease, neuropsychiatric symptoms, and Kayser-Fleischer rings. Treatment is copper chelation and zinc — removing the toxic metal, a strategy distinct from degradation or replacement.

03What goes wrong

Loss-of-function copper transporter leading to copper overload. ATP7B normally exports copper into bile; its deficiency causes copper to accumulate in the liver, brain, and cornea. The pathology is driven by the toxic copper overload (a downstream metal accumulation), not by a toxic protein aggregate and not by a simple enzyme-replaceable deficiency.

04How NIGHTINGALE routes it

mechanism: chelatable_metal not a degradation target

Recommended modality: chelator (remove excess copper — penicillamine, trientine) + zinc (block absorption)

NOT eligible (protein-level). The disease is copper overload due to a loss-of-function transporter; the correct intervention is copper CHELATION (remove the toxic metal) or zinc therapy (block absorption), not protein degradation or enzyme replacement.

05Treatment today

D-Penicillamine and Trientine (chelators); Zinc acetate (absorption blocker)

Penicillamine/trientine/zinc long-established for Wilson disease
Copper chelator + zinc · penicillamine 149.2 Da · trientine 146.2 Da · zinc acetate 183.5 Da

Chelators bind and promote urinary excretion of excess copper; zinc induces metallothionein to block intestinal copper absorption. Both lower body copper burden.
