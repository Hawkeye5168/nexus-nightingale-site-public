02What it is

Dengue is caused by dengue virus, an enveloped positive-sense RNA virus in the family Flaviviridae. There are four antigenically distinct serotypes, and the serotype is part of the target definition.

Infection with one serotype usually gives durable immunity to that serotype, but only incomplete cross-protection against the other three. A later heterotypic infection is the setting in which severe dengue is most likely. That is why a vaccine or antiviral claim based on one serotype is incomplete until pan-serotype coverage is shown.

The virus is transmitted primarily by infected female Aedes aegypti mosquitoes; Aedes albopictus is a secondary vector. Transmission is most efficient in urban and semi-urban settings. Dengue is not spread by casual contact, although blood-borne and vertical transmission matter for screening policy.

03What goes wrong

Symptomatic dengue usually begins with an abrupt febrile phase lasting two to seven days, with headache, retro-orbital pain, myalgia, arthralgia, rash and gastrointestinal symptoms. Many patients recover without a critical phase. The critical phase usually begins around defervescence, when plasma leakage may start.

Warning signs include abdominal pain or tenderness, persistent vomiting, fluid accumulation, mucosal bleeding, lethargy, liver enlargement and rising haematocrit with falling platelets. Severe dengue is defined by shock, respiratory distress, severe bleeding or organ impairment. The clinical priority is recognising the transition early and managing fluid balance carefully.

Antibody-dependent enhancement is one mechanism linking secondary heterotypic infection to severe disease: cross-reactive, sub-neutralising antibodies can facilitate Fcγ receptor-mediated entry into monocytes and macrophages. NS1 contributes to endothelial activation and vascular leak, and dengue proteins also antagonise interferon signalling through STAT2 and STING. These are mechanisms, not validated drug targets on their own.

04How NIGHTINGALE routes it

mechanism: viral replicationprevention: vector control and vaccinesresearch: direct-acting antiviralsnot a clinical recommendation

The target landscape is viral, not a human gene. The most developed direct-acting classes are NS5 RNA-dependent RNA polymerase and methyltransferase inhibitors, NS2B–NS3 protease inhibitors, and NS4B inhibitors. The E protein is mainly a vaccine and antibody target; NS1 is a pathogenesis and adjunctive-therapy target.

No direct-acting dengue antiviral is approved. The pipeline therefore treats the target classes as hypotheses with different evidence stages, not as a validated clinical route. A dengue antiviral has to act before the critical phase, which makes the diagnostic and treatment pathway part of the target product profile.

05Treatment today

Supportive care

standard
Fluid balance · warning-sign monitoring · early referral

There is no approved antiviral. Management is supportive: careful fluid replacement, monitoring for plasma leakage and shock, and recognition of the critical phase. NSAIDs and aspirin are avoided because of bleeding risk. The quality of supportive care is itself a reason a modest antiviral effect can be missed in a trial.

Dengvaxia (CYD-TDV)

restricted vaccine
Live attenuated tetravalent · three doses · serostatus-dependent

Approved for individuals 9 through 16 years of age with laboratory-confirmed previous dengue infection who live in endemic areas. It is not approved for people with unknown or negative serostatus; in seronegative people, vaccination was associated with an increased risk of severe dengue after subsequent infection.

Qdenga (TAK-003)

vaccine
Live attenuated tetravalent · DENV-2 backbone

Approved in the EU for individuals aged 4 and older without a serostatus restriction in the EU label. Efficacy varies by serotype, baseline serostatus and follow-up time. Takeda withdrew its US application in July 2023 after the FDA requested additional data.

Vector control and Wolbachia

prevention
Source reduction · larvicides · adulticides · population replacement

Vector control remains the foundation of prevention. The AWED trial in Yogyakarta reported 77.1% protective efficacy against virologically confirmed dengue using Wolbachia-infected Aedes aegypti; the effect is local and requires sustained deployment, community acceptance and regulatory oversight.

What this page does not do: it does not provide dose management, eligibility criteria, serostatus interpretation or an individual treatment plan. Approval is not access, and a vaccine is not a treatment for acute dengue.

06In the pipeline

  • NS5 polymerase and methyltransferase. The most developed direct-acting target class; nucleoside and non-nucleoside inhibitors have preclinical and early clinical evidence, but no approved dengue drug.
  • NS2B–NS3 protease and NS4B. Protease inhibitors and NS4B inhibitors are credible research routes; selectivity, resistance and pan-serotype activity remain open.
  • NS1 and host-directed adjunctive approaches. NS1 contributes to vascular leak; an adjunctive strategy would need to show benefit on progression, not just a biomarker.
  • Early intervention and pan-serotype coverage. A drug given after peak viraemia may be too late. The programme needs a diagnostic pathway and a primary endpoint that is patient-important.
  • Repurposing. Ivermectin, niclosamide, pyrimethamine and chloroquine have been studied, but none has demonstrated clinical efficacy in dengue. Preclinical activity is not a clinical claim.

07Source checks

What was checked for this release, and what remains open.

  • Identity: DENV-1 to DENV-4 polyproteins and the four serotype records are source-linked to UniProt and NCBI RefSeq. The page does not treat a genotype as a serotype.
  • Construct folds: DENV-1 NS2B/NS3 → PDB 3L6P, TM 0.9231 / RMSD 4.36 Å; DENV-2 methyltransferase → 3EVG, 0.9914 / 1.38 Å; DENV-3 NS5 → 5CCV, 0.8995 / 10.56 Å; DENV-4 construct → 3UC0, 0.8163 / 7.06 Å. These are construct-level template recoveries, not full-polyprotein predictions.
  • Provenance correction: 3UC0 is a P09866 construct, not Q2YHF2. The DENV-4 record is a construct-level template match, not a Q2YHF2-to-3UC0 resolution.
  • Assurance boundary: the four records are published with a template-recovery control; the native-versus-native scorer audit is recorded in the access-controlled assurance pack. No full-length DENV structure is claimed.
  • Sources: 33 source records, including WHO, CDC, FDA, EMA, ClinicalTrials.gov, UniProt, RCSB PDB and primary antiviral literature. Counts and trial totals are dated; they are not a live feed.
  • Open item: external clinical review is not complete. There is no approved dengue antiviral; vaccine efficacy varies by serotype and serostatus; the structural work is construct-level. The 322-study trial count is not a count of efficacy trials.
Amber items are open questions, not hidden caveats. A construct fold is not a full-polyprotein model, and a source-linked dossier is not a clinical validation.
