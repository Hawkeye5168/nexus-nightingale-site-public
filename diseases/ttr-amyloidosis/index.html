02What it is

Hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis is an autosomal dominant disease in which mutant transthyretin misfolds and forms amyloid deposits in nerves and heart, causing progressive neuropathy and cardiomyopathy. It is a toxic gain-of-function amyloidosis; approved therapies silence or stabilise the amyloidogenic protein, and selective degraders are the next frontier.

03What goes wrong

Gain-of-function / toxic aggregate. Mutant transthyretin (TTR) is destabilized and misfolds, dissociating from its native homotetramer and aggregating into amyloid fibrils that deposit in peripheral nerves and heart. This causes progressive sensorimotor/autonomic neuropathy and infiltrative cardiomyopathy. The amyloidogenic TTR is the toxic species.

04How NIGHTINGALE routes it

mechanism: toxic_gof AUTOTAC-eligible

Recommended modality: silencer (siRNA/ASO approved) + stabilizer + selective degrader

ELIGIBLE. Amyloidogenic TTR is a toxic gain-of-function aggregate — reducing or degrading the misfolded TTR is mechanistically appropriate. Approved therapies (patisiran, inotersen) already work by *silencing* TTR production; a selective degrader of amyloidogenic TTR is the same logic via a different route.

05Treatment today

Patisiran (Onpattro)

2018-08-10 (Alnylam, NDA 211172)
TTR mRNA silencer (RNA interference) · siRNA — MW not applicable

A small interfering RNA (siRNA) that degrades TTR mRNA, reducing hepatic transthyretin production and thereby lowering amyloidogenic TTR levels. It is NOT a small molecule and does not degrade the protein directly.

06In the pipeline

  • Inotersen (Tegsedi) — antisense oligonucleotide TTR silencer (approved 2018)
  • TTR stabilizers: tafamidis (Vyndaqel) — binds native tetramer, prevents dissociation (approved 2019)
  • Selective TTR degraders (PROTAC/AUTOTAC) — preclinical
  • CRISPR/Cas9 in vivo TTR gene editing (clinical, e.g. NTLA-2001)
