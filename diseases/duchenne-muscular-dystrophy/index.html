02What it is

Duchenne muscular dystrophy is the most common childhood muscular dystrophy, an X-linked disorder caused by dystrophin deficiency. Boys develop progressive muscle weakness from early childhood, losing ambulation in their teens and dying from respiratory or cardiac failure in young adulthood. Gene therapy and exon-skipping are the leading disease-modifying approaches.

03What goes wrong

Loss-of-function. Mutations in DMD abolish dystrophin, a large cytoskeletal protein that links the muscle fibre cytoskeleton to the extracellular matrix. Without dystrophin, muscle membranes are fragile and progressively degenerate, replaced by fibrosis and fat. This is a pure loss-of-function disease.

04How NIGHTINGALE routes it

mechanism: structural_lof not a degradation target

Recommended modality: gene therapy (micro-dystrophin) + exon skipping

NOT eligible. DMD is a loss-of-function disease — dystrophin is absent, not accumulated. Degrading any residual/truncated dystrophin would worsen the disease. Correct routes are dystrophin restoration: gene therapy (micro-dystrophin), exon skipping (to restore reading frame), and stop-codon readthrough.

05Treatment today

Deflazacort (Emflaza)

2017-02-09
Corticosteroid (anti-inflammatory, symptomatic) · 441.5 Da · PubChem CID 189821

Corticosteroid that slows muscle degeneration by reducing inflammation; prolongs ambulation by several years. Does NOT restore dystrophin.

06In the pipeline

  • Gene therapy: delandistrogene moxeparvovec (Elevidys) — AAV micro-dystrophin (FDA accelerated approval 2023)
  • Exon-skipping ASOs: eteplirsen (exon 51), golodirsen (53), casimersen (45), viltolarsen (53)
  • Stop-codon readthrough: ataluren (translarna) — conditional approval in EU
  • Utrophin upregulation (compensatory dystrophin homologue)
