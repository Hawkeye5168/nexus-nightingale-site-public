02What it is

Sickle cell disease is an autosomal recessive haemoglobinopathy caused by pathogenic variants in HBB. The canonical variant is HbS (p.Glu6Val), but HbSS, HbSC and HbS/beta-thalassaemia are different genotypes with different trajectories.

HbS polymerises when deoxygenated, deforming red cells and driving vaso-occlusion, haemolysis and chronic organ injury. The disease is not one uniform phenotype, and a genotype label is not a full prognosis.

This page is a research summary, not a diagnosis, eligibility decision or treatment recommendation. Diagnosis requires haemoglobin analysis and genetic confirmation.

03What goes wrong

HbS polymerisation reduces red-cell deformability and promotes adhesion, microvascular occlusion and ischaemia. Haemolysis releases free haemoglobin and arginase, reducing nitric oxide availability and contributing to endothelial dysfunction.

The consequences include acute pain, acute chest syndrome, stroke, priapism, splenic sequestration, renal disease, retinopathy, avascular necrosis, pulmonary hypertension and leg ulcers. A low crisis rate does not mean low organ risk.

Structural note: the pipeline used PDB 1A01, a haemoglobin construct, not the sickle red cell or its vascular interactions. The supervised reconstruction of that construct is not a model of vaso-occlusion and not a full-length cellular model.

04How NIGHTINGALE routes it

mechanism: HbS polymerisationdisease-modifying: hydroxyureacurative: HSCT / gene therapynot a clinical recommendation

Approved route: hydroxyurea and supportive care are the backbone for many patients; transfusion is used for selected acute and chronic indications; HSCT or gene therapy is considered only in the appropriate specialist setting.

Sickle cell disease is not a simple loss-of-protein story. HbS is a gain-of-function polymerisation problem; a generic degrader is not proposed. No route is a universal cure, and a crisis reduction is not automatically organ protection.

05Treatment today

Hydroxyurea

oral backbone
Raises foetal haemoglobin · reduces some vaso-occlusive events

Hydroxyurea increases HbF and has been associated with fewer pain crises and acute chest events. It requires blood-count monitoring and is not a cure. Access and adherence remain uneven.

Transfusion and iron chelation

acute and chronic use
Lowers HbS · stroke prevention · acute chest and priapism

Transfusion reduces HbS and prevents some strokes, but causes iron overload and alloimmunisation. Chronic transfusion requires chelation and monitoring; it is not a benign supportive measure.

Gene therapies

selected severe SCD
Exagamglogene autotemcel · lovotibeglogene autotemcel

Two one-time autologous gene therapies are approved in the US for severe SCD. Both require myeloablative conditioning, specialist transplant capacity and long-term follow-up. They are not off-the-shelf or universally available.

Haematopoietic stem-cell transplantation

selected patients
Curative intent · donor required

HSCT can be curative in selected patients, but graft-versus-host disease, infertility, infection and access are major limitations. It is not a universal standard.

Status changes matter: voxelotor was withdrawn after safety concerns and crizanlizumab has had regulatory restrictions. Do not treat an old approval announcement as current availability. This page does not provide dose management or eligibility advice.

06In the pipeline

  • Gene-editing and gene-addition durability. Long-term follow-up is still maturing; clonal safety, fertility and organ outcomes remain open.
  • Genotype-specific evidence. HbSC and HbS/beta-thalassaemia are under-represented in trials; a genotype-agnostic claim can be wrong for a substantial subgroup.
  • Organ outcomes. Stroke, renal, pulmonary and retinal outcomes need to be measured separately from pain crises.
  • Access and delivery. Newborn screening, hydroxyurea, transfusion and curative therapy are unequally available across the world.
  • Combination and sequencing. The optimal sequence after hydroxyurea, transplant or gene therapy is not established.
