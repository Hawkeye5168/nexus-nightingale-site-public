02What it is

Ebola disease is the umbrella term for illness caused by viruses in the genus Orthoebolavirus. The 2026 emergency is caused by Bundibugyo virus, not by the Zaire species that the approved vaccines and antibodies target.

BDBV is one of three orthoebolaviruses known to cause large human outbreaks, alongside Ebola virus (EBOV) and Sudan virus (SUDV). The genus also contains Taï Forest virus, Reston virus and Bombali virus. The species distinction is not taxonomic housekeeping: product labels, vaccine stockpiles and monoclonal antibodies are virus-specific.

The clinical syndrome overlaps with EBOV disease — fever, fatigue, gastrointestinal symptoms, bleeding in a minority, and multi-organ involvement — but the evidence for any product must be generated in the species causing the outbreak. A statement that “Ebola is preventable” or “Ebola is treatable” is incomplete unless it names the virus and the product.

Scope: this page is a research summary, not a diagnosis, treatment recommendation, eligibility decision or regulatory conclusion. Treatment decisions belong to a qualified clinical team and the current local label.

03The 2026 outbreak

The Democratic Republic of the Congo declared the outbreak in Ituri Province on 15 May 2026. By 12 November 2026 it had reported 11,197 confirmed cases and 5,471 deaths, with 3,045 people recovered and 1,676 hospitalised in isolation. Eight provinces have reported cases; Ituri and Nord-Kivu account for about 94% of confirmed cases.

Kenya reported a nine-case cluster with two deaths, and Nigeria reported one case in a traveller. Uganda’s linked outbreak was declared over in July 2026. A British healthcare worker was evacuated to the UK on 5 November 2026 and admitted to the Royal Free high-level isolation unit.

The response is constrained by insecurity, mobile populations, cross-border trade and refugee movements. Those are not background details: they determine whether contact tracing and isolation reach the people at risk. The case count is a reporting snapshot, not a stable epidemic curve.

Do not compare case-fatality rates as if they were controlled evidence. The DRC’s reported BDBV case fatality has been much higher than the ~10% in the Uganda cohort, but the groups differ in presentation, access and support. The difference cannot establish that any experimental treatment works.

04Diagnosis: the first assay missed BDBV

Acute diagnosis relies on molecular testing, with antigen and serology as complements. In the 2026 outbreak, the first deployed real-time RT-PCR cartridge was EBOV-focused and returned a negative result; broader testing at the Institut National de Recherche Biomédicale identified BDBV. WHO listed the first BDBV molecular test under its Emergency Use Listing on 2 July 2026.

The lesson is operational, not merely technical: a negative EBOV-only result is not a rule-out for BDBV. Pan-orthoebolavirus assays and sequencing are part of preparedness, and a diagnostic that is not validated for the circulating species can delay the response.

The dossier separates a CE-marked or EUL-listed test from a marketed diagnostic and separates a detection result from a clinical decision. No test in this release is presented as a standalone rule-out.

05The countermeasure gap

EBOV: licensed productsBDBV: no licensed productsupportive care is the foundationnot a clinical recommendation

Licensed for EBOV: Ervebo (rVSVΔG-ZEBOV-GP), Inmazeb (atoltivimab / maftivimab / odesivimab) and Ebanga (ansuvimab). These are not licensed for BDBV, and a label for one species does not create a genus-wide licence.

No BDBV vaccine or therapeutic has a marketing authorisation as of 15 November 2026. The response relies on supportive care, infection prevention and control, surveillance, contact tracing and community engagement. BDBV candidates — including ChAdOx1 BDBV, mRNA-1469 and the Tumaini and PARTNERS platform trials — are investigational; no BDBV efficacy result exists yet.

06What the structural evidence is — and is not

The NEXUS NIGHTINGALE structural layer was run on the EBOV glycoprotein accession Q05320: GP, 676 aa, 74,464 Da, Zaire ebolavirus strain Mayinga-76. The supervised fold resolved to PDB 2EBO and recovered the 222-residue GP2 core. 2EBO is a construct, not the full-length, membrane-anchored GP1,2 trimer.

That result is a construct-level recovery against a deposited template. It is not a prediction of the full 676-residue GP, and it is not a BDBV structure. No full-length BDBV GP structure was used. The BDBV deposits that do exist are mucin-deleted GP trimers, an NP C-terminal domain and a VP40 dimer; none is the full-length GP1,2.

Construct note: a high recovery score against a domain fragment can be true and still say nothing about the full protein or about a different species. The construct, the chain and the missing regions must be read with any structural number.

07What this release does not establish

  • No claim that any vaccine, antibody or small molecule treats or prevents BDBV disease.
  • No full-length BDBV GP structure and no BDBV-specific structural model.
  • No randomized BDBV efficacy result; the Uganda remdesivir experience is uncontrolled.
  • No independent Ebola specialist review has been completed. This is an internal draft for that review.
  • The case counts are a 15 November 2026 snapshot and will change.

A structural or source check is not a claim of clinical validation. The dossier names its gates and its limits so a specialist can attack the weakest points first.
