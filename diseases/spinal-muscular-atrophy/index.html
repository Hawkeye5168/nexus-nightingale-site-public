02What it is

Spinal muscular atrophy is an autosomal recessive motor-neuron disease caused by biallelic loss of SMN1. It is not one uniform disease: the exact genotype, SMN2 copy number and stage change the trajectory and the treatment discussion.

SMN1 encodes the survival motor neuron protein. SMN2 is a nearly identical paralogue that produces only a small amount of full-length SMN because of an exon 7 splicing difference. The SMN2 copy number is a modifier, not a deterministic prediction.

Diagnosis is confirmed by molecular genetic testing. Clinical appearance alone is not sufficient. This page is a research summary, not a diagnosis, eligibility decision or treatment recommendation.

03What goes wrong

SMN is required for snRNP assembly and motor-neuron maintenance. Reduced SMN produces cell-type-specific splicing defects, neuromuscular junction dysfunction and motor-neuron loss, with secondary muscle atrophy and weakness.

The disease is not confined to the motor-neuron cell body. Respiratory, bulbar, orthopaedic and nutritional complications determine much of the morbidity, and they are not captured by a single motor score.

Structural note: there is no full-length experimental structure of SMN in this dossier. PDB 4GLI is a crystal structure of the human SMN YG-dimer at 1.90 Å. The supervised pipeline reconstructed that construct; it is not a full-length model or a blind prediction. A domain structure is useful context, not a drug pocket.

04How NIGHTINGALE routes it

mechanism: loss of functionapproved: restore SMNresearch: next-generation restorationnot a clinical recommendation

Approved route: restore SMN with nusinersen, risdiplam or AAV9 SMN1 gene therapy where the label, age and eligibility criteria fit; supportive care remains essential.

SMA is a loss-of-function disease: SMN is absent or reduced, not accumulated. Degrading it would be the wrong direction. No degrader candidate is proposed. The three approved routes are not interchangeable, and none is a universal cure.

05Treatment today

Nusinersen

intrathecal ASO
SMN2 exon 7 inclusion · loading and maintenance dosing

An antisense oligonucleotide that increases full-length SMN from SMN2. ENDEAR and CHERISH provide randomized evidence in infantile- and later-onset SMA. The label requires platelet, coagulation and urine protein monitoring; repeated lumbar punctures are a real burden.

Risdiplam

oral small molecule
SMN2 splicing modifier · once daily

An oral SMN2 splicing modifier studied in FIREFISH and SUNFISH. FIREFISH used historical controls; the motor and survival results are supportive but not a randomized placebo comparison. Systemic exposure brings systemic adverse effects and interaction questions.

Onasemnogene abeparvovec

AAV9 gene therapy
One-time intravenous SMN1 cDNA · label-defined age and eligibility

A one-time AAV9 SMN1 gene-replacement therapy. Pre-existing AAV9 antibodies, liver injury, immune responses, cardiac events and durability are central uncertainties. It is not a one-time cure.

Supportive and multidisciplinary care

always relevant
Respiratory · bulbar · nutrition · orthopaedic · rehabilitation

Airway clearance, non-invasive ventilation, feeding support, scoliosis and contracture management, physiotherapy and family support remain essential. They are not optional extras to a drug.

What this page does not do: it does not provide dose management, eligibility criteria or an individual treatment plan. Approval is not access, and a motor milestone is not a complete functional cure.

06In the pipeline

  • Presymptomatic treatment. Newborn screening and early treatment can preserve motor units; the trials support earlier treatment, not guaranteed normal function.
  • Next-generation SMN restoration. Higher-dose, next-generation ASOs and new delivery approaches are research-stage; they need randomized functional and safety data.
  • Add-on biology. Myostatin-pathway and muscle-directed agents are being studied, but they are not established replacements for SMN restoration.
  • Outcome measurement. Respiratory and bulbar outcomes are under-measured relative to motor scales; a walking test is not the whole disease.
  • Access and delivery. Specialist centres, intrathecal capacity and gene-therapy infrastructure determine whether an approved therapy reaches the patient.
